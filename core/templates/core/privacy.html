Data Privacy Notice

AdventureWorks business system · Notice version {{ version }} · Effective 19 October 2026

This notice explains, in line with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173), its Implementing Rules and Regulations, and National Privacy Commission (NPC) issuances, what personal data this system processes, why, how it is protected, how long it is kept, and how data subjects may exercise their rights.

I. Introduction and scope

AdventureWorks is an internal business system used to browse and maintain sales, customer, product and purchasing records. This notice covers every account and every personal data record stored in or displayed by the system, regardless of the tab it appears under. It applies to employees and staff who are issued accounts, and to the individuals whose contact details appear in the business records.

II. Data controller and Data Protection Officer

The system is operated as an academic data governance project. The Data Protection Officer (DPO) for this system can be reached at joshua.vinson@benilde.edu.ph for any question, request, or complaint about personal data. The DPO oversees this notice, the record of processing, and the handling of data subject requests.

III. What personal data this system processes

  • Account data. Username, display name, assigned role group, sign-in events, and privacy acknowledgment records (the notice version accepted and the date and time it was accepted).
  • Change audit records. Every edit or deletion made through the table pages is recorded: the account, the date and time, the record touched, the old and new values, and the IP address the request came from. These records exist for accountability and security.
  • Contact data of individuals in the business records. Names, email addresses, telephone numbers, and postal addresses of customer, store, and vendor contact persons. These appear in the Customer profile, Sales, and Cost tabs.
  • Operational records connected to those contacts. Orders, purchase orders, and related transaction history that reference the individuals above.

This system does not process sensitive personal information as defined in Section 3(l) of the DPA (such as health, government-issued identification numbers, or religious or political affiliations). Should that ever change, the processing will be justified under Section 13 of the DPA and this notice will be updated first.

Transparency note: the demonstration database ships with Microsoft's fictional AdventureWorks sample data. The records describe no real individuals. They are still handled under every control in this notice, because the same rules apply the moment real data is loaded.

IV. Why we process it, and the legal bases

Processing relies on the criteria in Sections 12 and 13 of the DPA:

  • Legitimate interests. Operating, securing, and administering an internal business system, and keeping proof of account acknowledgment of this notice.
  • Contract and business operations. Maintaining the sales, customer, and purchasing records that the organization uses to serve its customers and manage suppliers.
  • Legal obligation. Retaining records where the law requires retention, and responding to lawful requests of authorities.

Personal data is never processed for automated profiling, and no data is sold.

V. How access is controlled (least privilege)

Access is role-based: each of the 17 department roles sees only the tabs and tables it needs, with separate view, change, and delete rights per table. All gates are enforced on the server for every page and action, and are covered by automated tests. Account and role management is restricted to the system administrator. No role can create records in this system.

VI. How personal data is protected

Technical and organizational measures include: passwords stored only as salted hashes; session-based authentication; access revoked through role groups rather than shared accounts; transport encryption (TLS) in production deployments; least-privilege access control as described above; and controlled, permission-gated table access. Access to the database file itself is limited to the system administrator.

VII. Sharing and disclosure

Personal data is not shared with third parties. Within the organization, it is visible only to roles whose work requires it. Data is disclosed to authorities only when the law requires it, and the disclosure is logged and limited to what is required.

VIII. Retention and disposal

Records are kept only as long as they serve a declared purpose:

  • Account data is kept while the account is active and is deactivated when access is no longer needed.
  • Privacy acknowledgment records are kept for accountability for as long as the account exists.
  • Change audit records are kept for twelve (12) months from the change, then disposed of.
  • Business records are kept for the retention periods that apply to the sales and purchasing functions.

When retention ends, data is disposed of by secure deletion or irreversible anonymization, and disposal is reviewed periodically so that kept data still matches a live purpose.

IX. Your rights under RA 10173

Every data subject has the following rights, without discrimination:

  • Right to be informed whether personal data relating to you is being processed, and why.
  • Right to object to processing based on consent or legitimate interests.
  • Right to access a copy of your personal data held by the system.
  • Right to rectification of inaccurate or incomplete personal data.
  • Right to erasure or blocking of data whose retention is no longer lawful or necessary.
  • Right to damages for violations of your privacy rights.
  • Right to data portability to receive your data in a structured, commonly used electronic format.
  • Right to lodge a complaint with the National Privacy Commission.

These rights are transmissible to your heirs and assigns as allowed by law, and are exercised free of charge, except where a request is manifestly unfounded or excessive.

X. How to exercise your rights

Send a request to the DPO at joshua.vinson@benilde.edu.ph. Requests are verified, then acted upon within fifteen (15) working days from receipt. If a request cannot be granted, the reason and the available remedy will be explained in writing.

XI. Security incidents and breach notification

If a personal data breach occurs, the response follows NPC Circular No. 16-03 on personal data breach management: the incident is contained and assessed, the NPC and affected data subjects are notified within seventy-two (72) hours of knowledge when the breach meets the notification thresholds, and affected individuals are told what happened and what they can do to protect themselves.

XII. NPC registration and accountability

This system is an academic project. Before any operational deployment with real data, the appropriate NPC registration and a Privacy Impact Assessment will be completed. Records of processing activities are maintained for the system, and this published, versioned notice is part of that accountability record.

XIII. Changes and versioning

This notice has a version number. Material changes are published as a new version. Your acknowledgment is recorded with the version and time on every successful sign-in, so after a version change you acknowledge the new text the next time you log in. Acknowledgment history is retained as evidence.

XIV. Contact and complaints

Questions or complaints go first to the DPO at joshua.vinson@benilde.edu.ph. You may also lodge a complaint directly with the National Privacy Commission through privacy.gov.ph.
